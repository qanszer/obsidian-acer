Yes, this is still completely doable, but we must optimize the layout to handle the back-to-back weekend sessions(Saturday morning to Sunday evening).

Because your muscles only have about 30 to 36 hours of recovery between those two workouts, running a standard full-body routine back-to-back will lead to severe fatigue and poor performance.

To fix this, we will use a Heavy/Light/Medium fatigue management strategy. We will place your most taxing leg movements on Wednesday and Sunday, giving your lower back and legs maximum time to recover.

## Optimized 3-Day Weekly Schedule

## Wednesday Evening: Day 1 (Heavy / High Volume)

_You have days of rest before and after this session, so we make it the hardest workout._

- Bulgarian split squat – 3 sets
- Incline bench press – 3 sets
- Pull-up – 3 sets
- Lying tricep extension – 3 sets
- Weighted sit-up – 3 sets

## Saturday Morning: Day 2 (Medium / Upper Focused)

_Fresh from a long break, we hit upper body hard and prime your legs without destroying them for Sunday._

- Romanian deadlift – 3 sets _(Hits hamstrings/glutes, leaving quads fresh for Sunday)_
- Incline bench press – 3 sets
- Pull-up – 3 sets
- Bicep curl – 3 sets
- Lu raise + lateral raise – 2 sets

## Sunday Evening: Day 3 (Light / Recovery Focused)

_Only ~30 hours after Saturday. We focus on quads and isolation movements to avoid system burnout._

- Bulgarian split squat – 2 sets _(Lower volume to protect the knees)_
- Lateral raise – 3 sets _(Low fatigue fatigue on joints)_
- Bicep curl – 2 sets
- Lying tricep extension – 2 sets
- Standing calf raise – 3 sets
- Weighted sit-up – 2 sets

## Why This Works for Your Exact Schedule

- Quad Protection: Bulgarian split squats are kept on Wednesday and Sunday evening, giving you a full 72 hours of rest in between.
- Overgrowth Prevention: By dropping the set volume slightly on Sunday, you trigger muscle protein synthesis three times a week without crashing your central nervous system.
