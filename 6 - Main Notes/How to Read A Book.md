### The 4 Levels of Reading

- **Elementary** — basic literacy, decoding words
- **Inspectional** — systematic skimming to grasp what a book is and whether it's worth reading; two sub-steps:
	- *pre-read* (title, preface, TOC, index, jacket, key chapters)
	- *superficial read* (a fast surface read straight through from start to finish, no stopping on hard parts)
- **Analytical** — full, thorough reading for understanding (the bulk of the book, rules below)
- **Syntopical** — comparative reading across multiple books on the same subject

### Analytical Reading — 11 Rules

**Stage 1: What is the book about as a whole?**

1. Classify the book according to kind and subject matter [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)
2. State what the whole book is about with the utmost brevity [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)
3. Enumerate its major parts in their order and relation, and outline these parts [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)
4. Define the problem or problems the author is trying to solve [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)

**Stage 2: What is being said in detail, and how?**  
5. Come to terms with the author by interpreting key words [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)  
6. Grasp the author's leading propositions by dealing with the most important sentences [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)  
7. Know the author's arguments by finding them in, or constructing them from, sequences of sentences [FAN AND FLAME](https://benjaminvrbicek.com/blog/2016/3/22/how-to-read-a-book-by-mortimer-adler-fan-and-flame-book-reviews)  
8. Determine which problems the author solved, and which not; decide which the author knew he'd failed to solve [The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/blogs/justin-taylor/adlers-three-stages-for-how-to-read-a-book-analytically/)

**Stage 3: Is it true? What of it?** (only judge _after_ completing 1–8)  
9. Don't criticize until you fully understand the book — no agreeing, disagreeing, or suspending judgment prematurely  
10. Don't disagree disputatiously — argue, don't quarrel  
11. Respect the difference between knowledge and opinion — give reasons for any critical judgment

### Rules for Critiquing (once you've earned the right)

- Show where the author is uninformed
- Show where the author is misinformed
- Show where the author is illogical
- Show where the author's analysis is incomplete

### Syntopical Reading (multiple books, same topic)

- Find relevant passages across all books, not just one
- Bring authors to common terms even if they use different words
- Establish a set of neutral questions all authors address
- Define the issues by ordering authors' answers on each question
- Analyze the discussion — order questions and issues to shed maximum light on the subject

### The one thing to actually _practice_

Everything above collapses into one habit: **before you finish a book, be able to answer — what's it about, what's the argument, is it true, so what?** That's the whole method. Apply it to your very next book instead of reading Parts 3–4 of Adler's own book.
