# Nihongo


### 8/20/26 - History and Culture

Daimyo - big land owners

**Feudal Era**
- Shogunate system - military dictatorship/government ruled by a Shougun
- Shougun - the supreme military commander who held more power than emperor
- Emperor > Shougun > daimyo (big landlords) > samurai > peasants (farmers, artisans, merchants)
- Kyoto was capital; Kamakura Shogunate was the first Shogunate
- Ashikaga clan overthrew Kamakura

**Sengoku Period**
- also known as "warring states"; period of constant war
- Oda Nobunaga unified most of japan; was not Shougun but was a powerful
- Toyotomi Hideyoshi came to power but also did not become Shougun; only continued Oda's legacy in the unification of Japan

**Imperial Regent**
- King Sejeong of Korea
- Hideyoshi wanted the neighboring countries to recognize Japan as their emperor

**Tokugawa Shogunate**
- Tokugawa Ieyasu completely unified Japan
- no war for 250 years
- the captial was moved from Kyoto to Edo (Tokyo)

**Edo Period (Tokugawa Period)**
- great stability, economic growth, and cultural development
- he achieved it because of sakoku policy (limited foreign contact)
- this period influenced the current Japanese culture

**Meiji Restoration (Meiji Ishin)**
- Commodore Matthew Perry ended the rule of Tokugawa, which made people realize that the Shougun cannot protect them from foreign power, that was why Emperor Meiji's political authority was restored
- abolition of the feudal system
- creation of modern military
- industrial development
- introduction to compulsory education
- adaption of western technology and institutions
- Japan became one of the strongest nations

#### Religion and Philosophy

1. Shinto - "The Way of the Gods"
2. Buddhism (bukkyou) - India > China > Korea > Japan; some japanese follow both religions; compassion, impermanence, meditation, enlightenment
	- means seated meditation; concentration, discipline, simplicity, mindfulness

Wa
- means harmony and peaceful cooperation which is considered the most important values in Japanese society
- concept encourages
	- respect for others
	- group cooperation
	- avoiding conflict
	- maintaining social balance

Cha no Yu
- japanese tea ceremony
- ceremonial preparation and serving of powdered green tea (matcha)
- matcha originated in china
- they believed that bitter tea will make you better
- hospitality, harmony, respect, purity, tranquility
- every movement has symbolic meaning

#### Customs

Bowing (Ojigi)
- eshaku (15 degrees) - casual greeting for friends, classmates, etc
- keirei (30) - respectful greeting in formal situations; sign of respect to superiors
- saikirei 45 - sincere gratitude, deep respect, serious apology
- dogeza - extreme apology; very rare today

Omotenashi
- Japan's spirit of wholehearted hospitality
- giving the best service without expecting anything in return
- izakayas, public resorts, hotels

Gift Giving (Okurimono)
- gift must be presentable
- importal social custom in japan
- offer gift with both hands, same with receiver

Hanami
- means flower viewing
- appreciation of cherry blossoms (sakura) in spring
- family picnics, celebrate spring, appreciate fleeting beauty of blossoms
- connected to buddhism

Matsuri
- japanese festivals from shinto beliefs
- processions, traditional dances, music, food stalls, fireworks, portable shrines (mikoshi)

Shougatsu
- japanese new year; january 1-3
- they prepare for new year a month or weeks ahead
- visiting shrines (hatsumode)
- sending new year's cards (nengajou)
- eating traditional new year's foods (osechi ryouri)
- otoshidama - giving money to children
